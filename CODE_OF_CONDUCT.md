# Code of Conduct

## Why this exists

Questions about origins touch identity, religion, family and politics. People
care deeply about them, and that's exactly why this project needs clear
rules for discussing them. Our aim is a place where a Pashtun, a Tajik, a
Hazara, a Baloch, a Punjabi, a Persian, a Jewish scholar of the Lost Tribes
traditions, or anyone else can examine the same evidence and be treated with
respect.

This code applies to everyone who takes part (contributors, reviewers and
maintainers) in every project space: issues, pull requests, discussions,
review comments, and any chat or event run under the project's name.

## Our principles

1. **Evidence first.** Claims are judged by their sources, not by who makes
   them, how confidently, or how many people agree. "I've always been told" and
   "everyone knows" are not evidence. They may be worth recording as
   *tradition*, labelled as such.
2. **No nationalism, in any direction.** This project doesn't exist to prove
   any people older, purer, more "original" or more entitled to a land than
   another. It doesn't take sides in present-day political or border disputes.
   Historical sources about such disputes can be cited like any other evidence.
3. **Respect for every community.** Pashtuns and their neighbours, past and
   present, are discussed with the same respect. That includes communities
   whose traditions the evidence doesn't support.
4. **Respect for traditions.** Genealogies, legends and religious traditions,
   including the Israelite descent tradition, matter to the people who hold
   them. We can examine whether evidence supports them historically without
   mocking them or the people who hold them.
5. **Changing your mind is a strength.** When the evidence changes, update the
   entry. Being corrected is part of the work, not a defeat.
6. **Assume good faith; ask before accusing.** Most mistakes are honest. Point
   them out kindly, with sources.

## Expected behaviour

- Support disagreements with citations, and engage with the other person's
  sources.
- Critique claims, not people.
- Describe views you disagree with in the way their best supporters would.
- Use the labels and wording of the studies you cite for present-day groups.
- Say "I don't know" or "the evidence is unclear" when that's true.
- Credit others' work: original researchers, translators and fellow contributors.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Claims or insinuations that any ethnic, tribal, religious or national group is
  superior, inferior, "purer", "fake", or less entitled to its identity.
- Slurs, stereotypes, or mockery of any people, religion, tradition, accent or
  dialect.
- Using genetic results to decide who "really" belongs to a people, or demanding
  others' ancestry or DNA results.
- Sharing anyone's personal information, including genetic data, without
  their explicit consent.
- Harassment, threats, intimidation, or sustained hostile argument after being
  asked to stop.
- Deliberately misrepresenting a source, inventing citations, or knowingly
  submitting false information.
- Turning project spaces into campaigns about present-day politics.
- Sexual language or imagery, and unwelcome personal attention.

## Reporting

If you experience or witness unacceptable behaviour, contact the maintainers
privately:

- **Email:** `[MAINTAINER CONTACT — to be added before the project goes public]`
- **Or** use GitHub's built-in **Report content** option on the comment or
  issue concerned.

Reports are kept confidential. Maintainers will respond as soon as they can,
normally within a week. Anyone involved in the report will not take part in
handling it.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may remove, edit or reject comments, commits, code, issues and
other contributions that break this code. Depending on how serious and repeated
the behaviour is, they may:

1. **Correct:** a private note explaining the problem and the expected behaviour.
2. **Warn:** a recorded warning, with consequences for continued behaviour.
3. **Suspend:** a temporary ban from interacting with the project.
4. **Ban:** a permanent ban from the project.

Fabricating sources, harassment, and sharing others' personal or genetic data
may lead directly to a suspension or ban.
